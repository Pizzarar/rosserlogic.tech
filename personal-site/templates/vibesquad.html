AGENTIC DEVELOPMENT ENVIRONMENT

{{ page.title }}

An app for running a squad of AI coding agents. You bring the ideas; it keeps them sorted, keeps each agent in its own lane, sends work to the right model, and checks the work before it gets to you.

In public beta on Windows.

How it works

  1. 1You + Brainstorm

    You've got ideas. You talk them through with your brainstorming buddy, and anything off-topic gets parked so nothing derails.

  2. 2Lead + Workers

    The buddy hands the plan to the lead, who splits it into separate git worktrees and sends each piece to the cheapest model that can handle it. Reviews go to a different model family, and each review has to say whether a change fixes the cause or only hides the symptom.

  3. 3Back to you

    When something needs your call, your phone pings. You say yes or no.

    ↺ back to step 1

Portraits: agent-drawn pixel art from VibeSquad's sprite generator.

How I got here

  1. "I was getting lost in the sauce."

    Too many agent chats, no idea who was doing what.

  2. "I wanted one place to see the team,"

    so I built my own team-first UI.

  3. "Then it needed structure":

    lanes, a lead per lane, workers under each lead.

  4. "Then lanes grew into Squads":

    one squad per project, each with its brainstorm buddy and lead paired up, and workers underneath.

  5. "Then it needed to feel like chat":

    tabs, split view, focus mode, pasted images.

  6. "Then the talking got its own flow":

    the brainstorm buddy, an idea inbox, restarting a long chat from a short summary.

  7. "Then I wanted it on my phone":

    phone app (PWA) and a desktop app.

  8. "Then it needed to install like a real app":

    a one-step Windows installer with its runtime bundled, a first-run checklist, and signed auto-updates on a stable or beta channel.

  9. "Then it needed to be a full dev environment":

    changes, files, terminal and scripts panels; a command palette (Ctrl+K); search across transcripts; rewind, fork and export a chat.

  10. "Then squads got an overview":

    a card per squad with a monogram emblem, a Needs-you count, the squad's team avatars and its lead's status.

  11. "Then the squad moved into an office tower":

    RiffBot (the squad's brainstorm buddy) and the Overlord (its lead) get a TV each in the sky above the tower. Below, each lane is an office floor with desks, a lead's desk and a shared elevator.

  12. "Then lanes learned to check their own work":

    each lane keeps a SPEC.md and a TASKS.json. Plans label every block "Root cause" or "Workaround", and a reviewer from a different model family has to answer whether a change fixes the cause or hides the symptom. A timeout bump, retry loop or swallowed error fails unless a follow-up replaces it.

  13. "Then it started auditing its own usage":

    a usage panel shows each provider's % used, reset time and burn rate, plus a split of tokens by role. On a schedule an Opus and a Gemini review read the numbers side by side and suggest routing changes; the prompt also hands them the previous review's advice to grade against the new numbers. Screens use sample data.

What it saved

  • MEASURED

    New leads in the VibeSquad lane now start at 3k–4k tokens of context (3 measurements, one lead), down from about 80k after reading a handoff. Escape From Idle leads still start at 87k–155k; trimming its rules is next.

  • MEASURED

    Workers get about 770 characters of team rules (about 200 tokens) instead of the lead-only rules. Before the split, one project's rules file put about 5.5k tokens into every worker start, and 68% of it was lead-only.

  • PROJECTED

    A restarted brainstorm reads a short lane summary (about 5k tokens) instead of carrying a chat of about 150k.

  • MEASURED

    A 41-task checklist of everyday jobs in an agent IDE: 21 passed at v0.1.1, 41 passed after two rounds of work.

  • MEASURED

    Across 426 merged units logged by the usage ledger in its first week (Nov 1-8, 2026), 420 needed one review round or none and 6 needed two or more; 9 (2%) were fixed after merge. One user, one machine, and 394 of the units carry no workflow tag, so this is not a comparison between workflows.
